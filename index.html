NEW · THE BOARD GAMES

You only see the backs of their pieces.

  • Hidden ranks. Both armies stand face down to each other. A scout can tell you weak, medium or strong; only a clash tells you for sure.
  • Sealed orders. Before the first move you open one of two envelopes: take a bridge, unmask a decoy, win three fights with one unit. Do it for extra points. The enemy has one too.
  • Three ways to win. Take the enemy command post, hold two of three objectives, or leave the other army so far behind that it withdraws.
  • Four commanders. Each computer opponent has its own character and setup, from the reckless attacker to the patient fortress.

A season nobody played

We ran a full season of the grand strategy game with only AI nations, as a test. No human players. These are its real headlines, shortened.

  1. Tick 360Continental Bloc answers the call of Atlantic Partnership and turns on the alliance SEU.
  2. Tick 372Two more alliances answer the call of SEU. The war spreads.
  3. Tick 381Sierra Union strikes back at the nation that hit it and takes North Sea Shelf.
  4. Tick 396A bloc stands: three alliances side by side against the coalition.
  5. Tick 400The coalition hits North Sea Shelf again and again, two alliances at a time, for fifteen ticks.
  6. The endOne nation finishes the Command Center at the prize. Alliance after alliance masses an assault, and one by one they break off. It holds for 12 ticks and wins the season.
THE GRAND STRATEGY GAME

Why one more turn

  • Every turn counts. One order too late and your border is open. One scout in time and you see the strike coming.
  • Nations remember. Hit a nation and it hits back. Grow too strong and the others form a coalition against you.
  • Alliances move the map. Big blocs fight each other, small alliances team up against the giant, and allies answer a call to arms.
  • One raid can start a war. Attack the wrong neighbour and its whole alliance comes for you.
  • You can come back. Lose a region, regroup, find allies, and strike while they are busy elsewhere.
  • The end is one battle. The season closes with a fight around the prize. Whoever holds it then, wins.

QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions

What is Terrastate?

Terrastate is a free grand strategy game in the style of Planetarion that runs in your web browser. You lead one nation against 300 AI nations on a world map of 25 theaters: build an economy, research, raise an army, join or found an alliance and fight for the Nexus, the one region that decides the season.

What are the board games?

Each world has a board game of hidden ranks for one opponent: Hidden Command (Terrastate), Veiled Legions (Romaterra) and Sealed Orders (Ironfront). You set up your army in secret on a 9 by 9 board, and the enemy only sees the backs of your pieces. Play against four computer commanders on four levels, or with two players on one screen. Before the battle you open a sealed order for extra points. A battle takes about 15 to 30 minutes. Free, in the browser, also on a phone.

Is Terrastate free?

Yes. Terrastate, Romaterra and Ironfront are completely free, and so are their board games and mini games. There are no payments, no ads, no account and no download. It is a non-commercial hobby project by Wim Kamphorst from the Netherlands.

Do I need to download or install anything?

No. Open playterrastate.nl/play.html in any modern browser and you are playing. You can install it as an app from the browser if you like, and it keeps working offline. Your saved game stays in your own browser.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. Terrastate, Romaterra and Ironfront are built for both phone and desktop, with a big END TURN button under your thumb on phones. The mini games work on both too.

How do you win a season?

Take the Nexus, build the Earth Command Center on it and hold it for 12 ticks. If nobody manages that, the Last Battle decides: the whole world marches on the Nexus and whoever holds it the longest wins. In Romaterra the prize is Roma and the Imperial Palace.

Does the computer cheat?

No. The AI nations pay exactly what you pay and only know what they have scouted. Rival alliances have a war council that picks targets, signs pacts, scouts and strikes together, but always with the same rules as you.

How long is a game?

You choose: a Quick season of 200 turns, Standard of 400 turns or Epic of 840 turns. It is turn-based, so you can end each turn yourself, or let it run automatically at 1x, 5x or 20x speed. Difficulty is Recruit, Veteran or Warlord.

I have never played a game like this. Where do I start?

New players get Field Training: five small tasks that teach you by doing, including a war game that explains your first battle. You can skip it at any time.

Is there multiplayer?

Not in the grand strategy games yet. The mini games have duels with a friend. What you play now is Season 0: single-player against AI nations, the training ground for the live world. The live world, one shared season with real people as the other nations, opens when 100 commanders have joined the waitlist on the Terrastate Discord.

What is Season 0?

Season 0 is the single-player phase of Terrastate, Romaterra and Ironfront. You learn the game against 300 AI nations while the community grows. When 100 commanders are on the Discord waitlist, the live multiplayer world opens. Players from Season 0 are marked as Founding Commanders.

What is new in version 8.0?

Update 8.0 (October 2026) is called Solid Ground, for Terrastate, Romaterra and Ironfront. The middle of the season comes alive with collapsing nations, coalitions and a race for the prize over the whole season; one turn report replaces the pop-ups; the phone layout uses cards with big buttons; Romaterra has a map of the Roman world and Ironfront a map of Europe in 1944; and many fixes, such as saved games and a clean season end.

What are the mini games?

Next to each grand strategy game there is a small mini game of its own: Terrastate: Conquer, Romaterra: Imperium and Ironfront: D-Day. It is one quick battle on a hex map: you give every unit an order, end the turn, and everyone moves at once. The grand strategy game is the main game; the mini game is a separate, shorter game with its own save. Free, in your browser and offline once played. All three are at playterrastate.nl/tactics/. What is Ironfront?

Ironfront is the third era of the game: Europe at war in 1944, on a map of real fronts and towns, with invented nations and leaders. You gather pounds, rations, coal, steel, petrol and radio gear, raise infantry divisions, tank battalions, artillery and strategic bombers, and win by holding 2 of the 3 Capital Sectors with a GHQ Bunker for 12 ticks. It also has its own battle layer: battle orders every tick, field works, radio intel with feints, and allied help that has to arrive in time. More about Ironfront.

What is Romaterra?

Romaterra is the same game set in the age of Rome. You gather denarii, grain, timber, iron, livestock and bronze, train legionaries, cataphracts, ballistae and war elephants, and fight to take Roma and hold the Imperial Palace.

What games is Terrastate like?

Terrastate is inspired by tick-based browser strategy games such as Planetarion, with the alliances, scouting and timed attacks of that genre, plus the map and diplomacy of a grand strategy or 4X game. The difference: you play against hundreds of AI nations, at your own pace, without waiting for other players.
