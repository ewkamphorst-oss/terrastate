NEW Patch 21.9.2: the game says what happened

For Terrastate, Romaterra and Ironfront. A new player played a season and told us where the screen said one thing and the game did another. All of it is fixed here.

Won the battle, land stays theirs: now you hear why

Beginner protection, a raised shield, a last land with its one-time emergency shield, or an ally whose larger army takes the land: the report names the reason and what to do next. The attack window warns you before you march.

Choose how much marches

The attack window offers Attack with half, three quarters or everything, and only the choices that your scout report says will take the land. The rest of your army stays home.

One estimate everywhere

The attack buttons, the Calculator and the attack itself now use the same estimate from your scout report. No more button that says attack while the order is refused.

Warnings that fit

An incoming army is called overwhelming only when the battle maths, your army and ground included, say so. Overview shows a battle on your land as an attack, not as safety. A battle that turns in the last round says what changed.

Your home sector, explained

The rule is about the land: nations cannot attack each other inside their own home sector. Lands outside it are open, both ways. The attack window says so.

Computer armies stop wasting themselves

They count only the armies that land together, turn back when the land they marched on was taken by someone stronger, and order the retreat from a hopeless battle with the same order you have. Hopeless full battles between nations dropped from about half to about one in ten.

Small things

Renew a pact where your neighbours are listed, with the turns left shown. Your influence and its income stand next to the pact offer. A few messages that named the wrong side or the wrong screen were corrected.

BETTER Update 21.9: nations that judge you

For Terrastate, Romaterra and Ironfront. We looked at how the great strategy games make their computer nations feel alive, and built our own version: no hidden numbers, no extra troops for the computer, every reason shown with something you can do about it.

Good deeds count

Until now a computer nation could only think worse of you. Now it also remembers when you helped it hold a land, traded with it, kept a pact to the end or threw back its army. Army › Defend a land shows, for every neighbour that has an opinion of you, its trust, its grudge and the reasons, with Make amends or Offer a pact right next to them.

Your name in your theater

The nations of your theater talk. Taking land and breaking pacts gives you a bad name there: neighbours refuse pacts and deals, and more of them join the pressure on you. Helping a neighbour hold and keeping pacts gives you a good name. It fades back to neutral over time.

A war director

The pressure on a leading nation no longer comes on a fixed clock. It builds while you lead undisturbed, you get a warning before it turns into armies, and after you lose a land you get a few quiet turns. The computer never gets extra troops or resources for it.

Every type has its own mark

The defender never strikes a nation first, and hits back when its walls hold. The opportunist strikes the nation whose army has marched away. Traders and diplomats come to their neighbours with deals and pacts. The attack window tells you which type owns the land.

Computer armies think again

Half of all computer attacks used to land on a defence far stronger than they expected, because the target's army came home while they marched. Now a computer general looks again on the last turn a recall is allowed and turns back, under the same rule as you. A weak nation with a grudge against you that cannot win now asks for peace itself; accept and the old grudge is laid to rest.

Fixed

The pressure on a leading nation had silently stopped in 21.8.6. It works again.
